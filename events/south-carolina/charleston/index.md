# Charleston, South Carolina

Founded in 1670, Charleston is South Carolina’s oldest city and a central hub of French Huguenot heritage in North America. Known for its rich colonial history, architectural preservation, and deep religious roots, the historic downtown district remains home to unique active congregations and annual commemorative traditions.

---

## Historic Churches & Traditions

### The French Protestant (Huguenot) Church

<img src="{{ '/events/south-carolina/charleston/media/media/image1.jpeg' | relative_url }}" class="img-fluid rounded shadow-sm d-block mx-auto mb-4" alt="The French Protestant Huguenot Church in Charleston, South Carolina">

Located in Charleston's French Quarter, the French Protestant (Huguenot) Church is the only remaining independent Huguenot congregation in the United States. The church maintains historical annual services in partnership with the French Huguenot Society of South Carolina:

* **Spring Service (Edict of Nantes):** Held each spring, this French-language service commemorates King Henry IV of Navarre’s 1598 decree granting French Protestants freedom of conscience and the right to read and interpret New Testament scriptures.
* **Fall Service (Revocation of the Edict of Nantes):** Commemorates the 1685 Revocation of the Edict of Nantes and honors the memory of the French Protestants who fled persecution and settled along the American coast.

* **Website & Annual Events:** [French Huguenot Church Annual Events](https://www.huguenot-church.org/annual-events.html)
