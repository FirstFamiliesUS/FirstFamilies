# Delaware County, Pennsylvania

Formed in 1789 from part of Chester County, Delaware County holds a central place in the early settlement of Pennsylvania and Quaker history. Bordering the Delaware River, the area was the site of William Penn's first landing in Pennsylvania in 1682 and served as a foundational community for the Society of Friends.

---

## Historic Sites & Quaker Heritage

### Chester Friends Meetinghouse

<img src="{{ '/events/pennsylvania/delaware/media/media/image1.jpeg' | relative_url }}" class="img-fluid rounded shadow-sm d-block mx-auto mb-4" alt="Chester Friends Meetinghouse in Chester, Pennsylvania">

The first recorded meeting of the Society of Friends in the province of Pennsylvania took place in Chester at the house of Robert Wade in 1675, with William Edmundson—the founder of Quakerism in Ireland—in attendance. 

In 1682, Chester Friends agreed to meet at the Chester Court House (then known as the House of Defense). Land was purchased on the west side of present-day Edgemont Avenue in January 1687, and the original meetinghouse was completed in 1693, where William Penn frequently spoke.
