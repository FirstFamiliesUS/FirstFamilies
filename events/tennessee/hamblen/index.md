# Hamblen, Tennessee

Situated in East Tennessee between Cherokee Lake and the Great Smoky Mountains, Hamblen County combines deep regional history with rich Appalachian heritage. From early frontier settlements and Civil War encampments to historic churches and community gathering places, the county spans key historic towns including Morristown, Russellville, and Whitesburg.

---

## Historic Towns & Heritage Sites

### Russellville, Tennessee

<img src="{{ '/events/tennessee/hamblen/media/media/image1.jpeg' | relative_url }}" class="img-fluid rounded shadow-sm d-block mx-auto mb-4" alt="First Baptist Church of Whitesburg">

Founded in 1784 by George Russell via a land grant in Greene County, North Carolina, Russellville is an historic community along U.S. Route 11E situated between Whitesburg and Morristown.

During the Civil War, Confederate Lieutenant General James Longstreet established headquarters at the **Nenney House** following the Battle of Bean's Station in December 1863, utilizing Russellville as winter quarters for his army (1863–1864). The house stands today as **The General Longstreet Museum**.

Other historic structures from the 1863–1864 winter encampment include:
* **Hayslope:** Originally built as a tavern with a red door by early settlers, it served as headquarters for General Lafayette McLaws.
* **Taylor Plantation:** Headquarters for General Joseph B. Kershaw.
* **Bethesda Presbyterian Church:** Used as a Civil War hospital and listed on the National Register of Historic Places; its cemetery holds many wartime burials, including 80 unidentified soldiers.

* **Reference:** [Russellville Wikipedia Entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russellville,_Tennessee)
