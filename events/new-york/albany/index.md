# Albany, New York

As the capital of New York and one of the oldest surviving European settlements from the original thirteen colonies, Albany holds a pivotal place in American history. First settled by the Dutch in 1614 as Fort Nassau (and later Fort Orange/Beverwijck), the city became a central hub for trade, regional governance, and colonial settlement in the Hudson River Valley.

---

## Lineage & Historical Societies

### Dutch Settlers Society of Albany

Founded in 1924 during the tercentenary celebration of the city's settlement, the Dutch Settlers Society of Albany works to preserve the memory, genealogical records, and cultural history of the early Dutch inhabitants of New Netherland and the Upper Hudson Valley.

* **Membership Eligibility:** Open to individuals at least 18 years of age who can prove direct descent from a person residing in Fort Orange, the Colony of Rensselaerswijck, or the Village of Beverwijck before **1665** (or in Esopus/Kingston prior to May 16, 1661). Associate memberships are also available for those interested in supporting the society's mission.
* **Publications & Resources:** The Society publishes a historical *Yearbook* containing original research, translations of 17th-century Dutch records, and colonial genealogies.
* **Website:** [Dutch Settlers Society of Albany Membership](https://www.dutchsettlerssociety.org/membership)
